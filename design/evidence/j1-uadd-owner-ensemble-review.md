## Owner precedents

- Rune owns the UADD method and compiler law: adversarial challenge, complete
  finding disposition and a later explicit acceptance decision over the same
  version. It is not a runtime truth owner.
- Moira owns attributable ensemble deliberation, findings and dispositions,
  revision-bound human-gate requests and responses, and durable replay.
  `HumanGateResponseV1` receives verified authority facts from Aegis rather than
  trusting the terminal submission.
- Aegis owns command authority. Evidence of a subject or decision does not
  itself grant authority.
- Vellum owns the human-readable decision packet and rationale material.
- Pantheon's qualified strict J1 receipt identifies the exact delivery but
  grants no human decision or task effect. Pantheon is the effect-free composer
  and presenter.
- Chronos review V10 alone owns the task-status effect and already binds an
  expected revision, task, attempt, disposition, evidence and stable invocation
  identity with lookup for lost replies.

## Options

### 1. Moira-owned typed UADD sub-contract — recommended

Moira adds a versioned UADD request, challenge receipt, finding-disposition set
and response composition adjacent to its generic human gate. Pantheon only
verifies and composes owner receipts before preparing a Chronos request.

Pros: fits the existing deliberation and human-decision owner; reuses durable
replay, findings and Aegis-injected authority; prevents Pantheon from becoming a
truth owner; gives unrelated generic approvals no path into UADD.

Cons: requires a new versioned Moira owner surface and qualification across
Moira, Aegis, Vellum, Pantheon and Chronos; Moira's current dirty worktree must
be preserved or isolated before implementation.

### 2. Inflate the generic Moira human gate

Encode UADD challenge and finding facts only as digests in the existing generic
request and accept ordinary `Approved` as the response.

Pros: fewer new public types and superficially less implementation.

Cons: loses the distinction between challenge and acceptance, permits unrelated
approval substitution, hides terminal challenge semantics and makes correctness
depend on caller convention. This weakens the canonical UADD method and is
rejected.

### 3. Chronos owns the UADD packet

Put UADD evidence and response semantics beside the task transition.

Pros: close to the eventual effect and its revision fence.

Cons: mixes acceptance-method truth with task-state ownership, duplicates
Moira's gate and findings, and makes the effect owner judge its own evidence.
Not recommended.

### 4. Pantheon owns the UADD receipt

Persist a Pantheon-specific acceptance receipt and map it to Chronos.

Pros: easiest local composition and presentation.

Cons: converts an effect-free adapter into a runtime truth owner, duplicates
Moira, and creates a boundary leak. Rejected.

### 5. Create a new component

Introduce a dedicated acceptance-evidence service.

Pros: clean isolation if UADD later becomes a substantial independent domain.

Cons: adds topology, identity, durability and operations before the existing
owner has proved inadequate. Premature for this bounded slice; retain as an
evidence-driven maturity option.

## Proposed version-one stable core

Moira owns versioned typed UADD evidence adjacent to, but not substitutable
with, its generic human gate. Exact public names remain an owner decision. The
contract must preserve these semantics:

1. The request binds the named acceptance principal or explicit authorized
   delegate policy, strict J1 receipt, task attempt, challenged Vellum material,
   criteria, threat inventory, policy and decision packet.
2. Per-attempt challenge records bind applicable STRIDE category, attempted
   route, protected effect, content-safe evidence and outcome. A separate
   terminal status distinguishes `Conclusive`, `Inconclusive` and `Unavailable`.
3. Every current-slice blocker is resolved against a new strict receipt after
   repair. Adjacent findings retain durable Chronos work linkage, owner,
   evidence and priority but are not acceptance evidence.
4. A typed UADD response exists only when the exact generic human response,
   request, challenge, finding set, strict receipt and authority subject match.
   An unrelated `Approved` cannot enter the composition.
5. Actor roles remain separately attributable. The adversary and acceptor are
   the named user or an authorized delegate only under the request's explicit
   owner-issued policy; agent or presenter activity cannot impersonate either.
6. `Approved` maps to Chronos `Accepted`; `ChangesRequested` maps to
   `RevisionRequired`; `Deferred` remains held in the Maybe Machine; `Rejected`
   remains a typed non-mutating UADD outcome until an explicit owner contract
   grants a different effect.
7. The Chronos invocation identity binds the typed UADD response, strict
   receipt and expected task revision. An unknown reply permits lookup of that
   exact invocation only, never blind retry or reuse after evidence changes.
8. RED/USE and golden-signal records expose content-free identities, phase,
   duration and outcome/refusal codes. They do not record result bytes, attack
   payloads, rationale text, credentials or authority secrets. Until Oculus
   owns and receives those signals, the contract states
   `oculus_published = false` rather than claiming observability.

Any repair that changes the delivered version invalidates the prior strict
receipt and challenge. The design may mature by adding owner-proved capability;
it must not weaken these invariants to obtain an executable projection.

## Proposed equitable stakeholder ensemble

The advisory SME roles are not the stakeholder ensemble. The proposed
accountable ensemble is:

- Brian, named human sponsor and acceptance principal, with the decisive human
  gate and standing to represent the bounded single-user operator experience;
- Moira owner, accountable for typed UADD evidence and the human gate;
- Chronos owner, accountable for the task effect and reconciliation;
- Aegis owner, accountable for authority and delegation facts;
- Vellum owner, accountable for exact human-readable material identity;
- Pantheon composition owner, accountable for effect-free verification and
  presentation;
- an affected operator/end-user advocate, represented by Brian only for this
  bounded single-user slice and requiring reassessment when the audience grows;
- independent security/adversarial and live-verification/audit challengers,
  with challenge and dissent standing but no human-acceptance authority; and
- Oculus/observability ownership as a consulted stakeholder for the telemetry
  contract, without allowing telemetry availability to redefine acceptance.

Equity means every owner can refuse misuse of its boundary, challengers can
retain attributed dissent, and the human acceptance principal is not outvoted
by agent or owner consensus. No role's model output is silently promoted to
human judgment.
